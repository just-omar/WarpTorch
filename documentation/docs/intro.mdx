### What makes this comparison interesting?

**Progression of technology:** The table shows how propulsion technology evolved from walking speed to achieving Earth orbit, then interplanetary travel. But there's a massive gap — we haven't progressed beyond Voyager speeds in 50 years.

**The interstellar gap:** Current spacecraft (Voyager, New Horizons) would need 75,000 years. Even theoretical fusion propulsion (Project Daedalus) would take 36 years. There's simply no practical way to travel between stars with conventional propulsion.

**Near-term hope:** Breakthrough Starshot (laser sails) might reach Proxima Centauri in 21 years, but can only carry gram-scale probes — not humans.

**Warp drive advantage:** Warp drives at 10x light speed would reach Proxima Centauri in 5 months — making interstellar travel comparable to a Mars mission with current technology. This transforms interstellar travel from "impossible" to "difficult but feasible."

Traditional space travel works like this: you burn fuel to push yourself forward. But there's a limit — you can't go faster than light, and even approaching that speed requires impossible amounts of energy.

**Warp drives take a different approach.**

Instead of pushing the ship, they **move space itself**. Imagine you're on a surfboard:
- **Traditional propulsion:** You paddle with your arms (slow, tiring)
- **Warp drive:** The ocean moves beneath you while you stand still (fast, efficient)

### The Alcubierre Solution

In 1994, physicist Miguel Alcubierre proposed a solution: create a "bubble" of flat space that moves through spacetime itself.

**How it works:**
1. **Compress** space in front of the ship
2. **Expand** space behind the ship
3. The bubble moves through spacetime, carrying the ship inside
4. The ship doesn't move — space moves around it

**The result:** You could theoretically travel faster than light without breaking any laws of physics.

### Why haven't we built one yet?

There are still challenges:
- **Energy requirements:** Early Alcubierre designs needed more energy than exists in the observable universe
- **Exotic matter:** Some theories require materials with negative mass that may not exist
- **Stability:** Keeping the warp bubble stable during travel is extremely difficult
- **Scale:** Creating spacetime curvature requires engineering on planetary scales

**But progress is happening.** Newer metrics (like the Lentz soliton, 2021) show that positive energy might work, reducing energy requirements from "impossible" to "extremely difficult but theoretically possible."

## How does it work? ⚙️

### Key concepts (simply explained)

#### 1. What is a "spacetime metric"?

Imagine space as a rubber sheet. If you put a heavy ball (a star) on it, the sheet curves. This is "spacetime curvature."

A **metric** is the mathematical description of how space is curved. For a warp drive, we create a special metric that "squashes" space in front of the ship and "expands" it behind.

#### 2. The Alcubierre Warp Bubble

This is the most famous type of warp drive (Miguel Alcubierre, Mexican physicist, 1994).

**How it works:**
- The ship is inside a "bubble" of flat space
- Space in front contracts
- Space behind expands
- The bubble moves faster than light (but the ship itself is at rest!)

#### 3. The energy problem

According to Alcubierre's theory, you need **negative energy** — which doesn't exist in nature.

But modern metrics (like the **Lentz soliton, 2021**) show that you can use **positive energy** — much more realistic!

WarpTorch lets you explore all these metrics and see how much energy is needed.

## Usage examples 💻

### Example 1: Your first warp bubble

<BrowserWindow>

```python title="first_warp.py"
from core.metrics.alcubierre import get_alcubierre_metric
from core.solver.energy import get_energy_tensor
from core.visualizer import plot_3d

# Create a warp bubble moving at 1.5x speed of light
metric = get_alcubierre_metric(
    grid_size=(1, 64, 64, 64),  # grid size
    v=1.5,                      # velocity (1.5c)
    R=6.0,                      # bubble radius
    sigma=2.0,                  # wall thickness
    device="cuda"               # GPU acceleration
)

# Calculate how much energy is needed
energy = get_energy_tensor(metric)

# Visualize in 3D
plot_3d(metric)
```

</BrowserWindow>

**Result:** In 5 seconds you'll see a beautiful 3D visualization of a warp bubble!

### Example 2: Explore different metrics

<BrowserWindow>

```python title="compare_metrics.py"
from core.metrics.alcubierre import get_alcubierre_metric
from core.metrics.lentz import get_lentz_metric
from core.solver.energy import get_energy_tensor

# Alcubierre - classic warp (needs negative energy)
alcubierre = get_alcubierre_metric(v=2.0)
alcubierre_energy = get_energy_tensor(alcubierre)

# Lentz - modern metric (positive energy!)
lentz = get_lentz_metric(v=2.0)
lentz_energy = get_energy_tensor(lentz)

# Compare - how much energy is needed?
print(f"Alcubierre: {alcubierre_energy.min():.2f} (negative!)")
print(f"Lentz: {lentz_energy.min():.2f} (positive = more realistic)")
```

</BrowserWindow>

**Result:** Compare different warp metrics and understand which is more realistic!
